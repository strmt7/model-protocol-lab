    Negation probe — design round (ADJUDICATED: BUILD, with binding amendments)¶

    Round summary (coordinator #36, design round only — nothing built)¶

    • Tree at main 960b70dcde87 (cycle 211 closed green, 8,402 tests, per the round brief). This round was READ-ONLY except for this doc and the briefs; no suite was run, no push was made, no tag was cut or discussed.
    • Specialists (blind to each other, then audited): researcher (~/workspace/agents_out/negation/research.md), red-team A (redteam_a.md), red-team B (redteam_b.md), verifier/claims auditor (verifier.md). All four briefs landed.
    • Convergence and divergence, in one paragraph: both red teams independently returned NO-BUILD for the loose sketch, and the verifier sustained NO-BUILD for the researcher's narrowed instrument as the researcher specified it — its §1 "iff" is false, its §3.4 operating characteristics story is backwards, its winner readout must be dropped rather than demoted, and its floors, echo decomposition, and scope justification were unfrozen. The coordinator concurs with every one of those judgments about the brief as specified. The verdict below is nevertheless BUILD, for a precise reason stated in §1: every defect the verifier names is corrected inside this doc, by amendment to the same instrument — same population, same arms, same scorer, same joint-rule shape — with the one genuinely quantitative correction (the decision threshold) derived by the coordinator's own execution in §7 and frozen here. The build cycle implements THIS doc, not the research brief. Any deviation from a frozen item in this doc (arms, rule, threshold, vocabulary, echo set, claim language) is not an implementation detail: it voids this verdict and requires fresh review. This is the distinction from the payload round's F6 rule: there the amendments replaced an undefined instrument with a different one (undefined payload → frozen tables, causal → descriptive); here they correct the decision rule and the claim language of an instrument whose population and arms were already frozen and executable.

    Verdict: BUILD (as amended in this doc)¶

    • Verdict: BUILD — the narrowed instrument specified in §2–§6, under the binding amendments in §1.1.
    • What the verdict is NOT: it is not a build order for the research brief, not for the loose "negation sensitivity" sketch, not for any winner headline, and not for the borrowed 6/2 floors. All four are NO-BUILD and are removed in §8.

    1.1 Binding amendments (each incorporated below, none left open)¶

    1. Claim narrowed and made one-directional. No "iff", no "negation-invariance is falsified", no "negation sensitivity" as a headline, no causal "negation changes verdicts" sentence. The section's only falsification-class label is negation_drop_observed, defined exactly in §2.4 as a sufficient pattern detector, with its named rival patterns printed beside it.
    2. Decision threshold re-derived from this panel's own operating characteristics (§7.3), not borrowed. The rule is D ≥ 8 and J ≥ 5 (definitions in §2.4). The borrowed placement/canonical-form floors (6/2) are REMOVED: the coordinator reproduced the verifier's finding that (6,2) fires on a random labeller 58% of the time. At (8,5) the random-labeller fire rate is ~4%, the no-effect competent-candidate fire rate is 0.7%, power at a 0.5 drop rate is 84%, and power at a 0.25 drop rate is only ~21% — the panel is powered for large drop rates and weak for small ones, and the section must print exactly that (§5).
    3. Winner readout DROPPED in v1, not demoted. No per-arm selection, no winner headline, no winner-vs-abstention readout. Per-arm pass rates and the joint pattern are the only readouts. Grounds: the numform settlement (experiments stage docstring, §7.1) forbids selection over the synthetic set, canonical-form's winner_headline is a total function of two arms with no three-arm extension, and NUMFORM_DESIGN itself records that over 16 values an all-pass selection readout "carries almost no information per unit of validator surface". Any future selection claim must first overturn that settlement with argument; it may not be added at build time.
    4. Frozen negation_echo decomposition (§4.2). The echo diagnostic is enumerated, validator-mirrored, and printed beside — never inside — the byte-exact headline. Without it the negated-arm rates are uninterpretable (a perfect comprehender answering "not second" scores 0/16 on the negated arm); with it, that pattern is labelled as echo, not as negation-drop.
    5. Antonym bundle disclosed in the claim itself (§2.2). The negated arm differs from direct in two lexical moves (negation token + larger→smaller antonym swap). The claim is therefore "antonym-negated definite-description discordance", and the section states the bundle wherever the headline appears. The flipped arm is a necessary competence gate, not a control: flipped-pass is necessary, not sufficient, for attributing a negated failure to negation processing, and wording × position interaction remains an unexcluded rival, printed as such.
    6. Scope justification (§6). Semantic instruction variation is deliberately excluded scope for surface-noise (see §6); this probe re-enters that scope for one frozen, derivable-ground-truth criterion variation, for the reasons in §6 — not for paraphrase generally.
    7. Open points decided, not deferred (§2.5, §3, §4): the 2×2 factorial completion is v2; the descriptive alt-negated arm is kept as a non-decision sensitivity only; the record shape is canonical multi-cell (one record per candidate × case, one cell per arm); the stage screen detail is negation_screen_failed, bound to the pre-generation audit screen defined in §4.3.

    1. Why the loose sketch was NO-BUILD (fatals, each executed)¶

    F1 — No single arm pair diagnoses negation¶

    Red-team B's executed truth table over the seed-0 generated compare set: a larger-default candidate (always answers the larger label) passes direct and negated 16/16 with zero direct–negated discordance, and is indistinguishable from a smaller-blind-lexical candidate on every per-arm pass count and every pair discordance. An always-first candidate yields maximal direct–flipped discordance (16/16) with zero negation content (red-team A E5). The direct–negated pair therefore cannot be the headline, and the direct–flipped pair cannot be a control: it measures antonym/criterion following, position bias, and base-task weakness jointly. Only the per-case triple pattern (pass direct, wrong-label on negated, pass flipped on the same case) diagnoses the negation-drop pattern — and only as a sufficient detector, never as an "iff" (see §2.4: larger-default is an undiagnosed rival pattern, not an acquittal; it is visible in the flipped pass rate printed beside the decision, never hidden).

    F2 — Byte-exact scorer × negated answer form (reporting fatal)¶

    The verdict is byte-exact (core.py:797: exact_match = status == "completed" and answer == case.expected), conjoined with boundary presence in stage cells. A semantically correct negated answer ("not second" for expected "first") therefore fails at every scorer-lens rung — the ladder has no label-strip operation (scorerlens.py: "label_prefix": "no label-strip operation exists") — and numform's own decomposition buckets it as non_label, the same class as gibberish (coordinator execution, §7.2). Wrong-label-only counting in the joint rule neutralises this for the boolean (echoes cannot fire it), but without a frozen negation_echo subclass the negated-arm rates stay uninterpretable and the perfect comprehender still scores 0/16 on the arm. That is why amendment 4 is binding and why the section must never print a negated-arm pass rate without its echo share beside it.

    F3 — The negated arm bundles treatments; length has no control¶

    Direct (79 chars) vs flipped (80) vs "not smaller" (84) vs "not the smaller one" (92) vs "not less than the other" (96) — red-team B's executed character deltas. The negated arm is longer by construction, and no length-matched negated control exists that is not itself a treatment (verbosity territory). Length is therefore a reported rival, not a controlled one: per-case id_length_delta is printed, the descriptive alt-negated arm (longer still) provides a length-gradient check, and a systematic wrong-label pattern (not mere failure) is the only pattern the section may attribute — with the length rival printed beside it, never argued away. Token-count delta is UNEXECUTED in this round (the tree has no frozen local tokenizer; only backend.encode exists) and must not be claimed in the build doc; character delta and render-id length delta are the reported quantities.

    F4 — Winner re-derivation over this panel is forbidden by settlement¶

    See amendment 3. The three-arm winner rule in the research brief is not a total function over three arms, and the settlement it would violate is explicit in the tree (experiments numform stage docstring: "No selection, no winner, no flip against the primary run: the synthetic set is a different contest from the user's fixtures, and a winner over 16 generated values per task would invite exactly the headline the design forbids"). Dropped, not demoted.

    F5 — Borrowed floors fire on noise (new fatal for the narrowed design)¶

    Coordinator reproduction (§7.3): the researcher's 6/2 rule fires on a random labeller (independent 0.5 labels per arm) 58% of the time, and the researcher's §3.4 operating-characteristics story is backwards (it claims the rule "fires only on a fairly lopsided pattern"). A falsification rule that fires on noise more often than not is not a conservative label; it is a broken instrument. The derived (8,5) rule in §2.4 replaces it in full. Borrowed floors are forbidden for every rule in this panel.

    Non-fatals, adjudicated¶

    • Conditional equivalence survives. "Not the smaller" ≡ "larger" given a ≠ b, by the pair-closed complement rule (§2.3). The article form beats the bare form (the verifier's §6.1: the bare form's own ¬(x<y) ⟺ x≥y concedes the equality ambiguity). Ties are not a population defect: the generator enforces a ≠ b (numform.py:550) and the validator re-derives it; an equal pair fails the stage closed (§4.3), never gets tie-broken. Refuted attacks (red-team B R1–R5): no-equivalent-phrasing, operand-order scoping change, context-capacity threat (max delta +17 chars), single-digit breakage, ties-as-population-defect — all refuted by execution and not carried forward.
    • Scope (red-team A M3) stands as a requirement, not a fatal. Addressed by §6.
    • Form-crossing (red-team A M1) is settled, not open. Plain form only in v1; form-crossing is a v2 panel with its own claim. Budget arithmetic follows from the frozen record shape (§3.3).
    • Degenerate populations (red-team A M4) are pinned, not open. Distinct-only, direct-pass conditioning, per-stratum/per-band reporting (§3).

    2. The instrument (frozen)¶

    2.1 Home and frozen base¶

    The home is the numform compare task, plain form only. Its frozen template is model_protocol_lab/numform.py:121-127 (compare entry):

    "Which of these two numbers is larger? Reply with only the word first or second.\n{a}\n{b}"

    Compare is the one numform task whose expected answer is a form-invariant label (numform.py:596-603, expected_answer: compare returns expected_label, never a rendered number). The generator constructs compare pairs with a != b enforced (numform.py:550), records which side is larger (larger_first, lines 572–576), and decomposes a compare cell into exact_pass / wrong_label (the other label) / non_label (numform.py:840-863, cell_decomposition). That decomposition is load-bearing: wrong_label on the negated arm is the negation-drop signature, non_label is a different failure, and negation_echo (§4.2) is a frozen subclass of non_label — never pooled into wrong_label, never rescuing a flip into a pass.

    2.2 Frozen instruction strings (v1 arms)¶

    Operands are rendered in plain form only and substituted for {a} (first line) and {b} (second line), exactly as numform does. The reply contract — "Reply with only the word first or second." — is byte-identical in every arm, so the answer vocabulary never varies. Operand order is identical across the arms of one case — only the instruction sentence changes.

    Arm Frozen instruction (operands appended, one per line) Expected answer
    direct Which of these two numbers is larger? Reply with only the word first or second. label of the larger operand
    negated (headline) Which of these two numbers is not the smaller? Reply with only the word first or second. label of the larger operand — the same label as direct
    flipped Which of these two numbers is smaller? Reply with only the word first or second. label of the smaller operand — the opposite label to direct
    negated_alt (descriptive only, §2.5) Which of these two numbers is not smaller than the other? Reply with only the word first or second. label of the larger operand — never a decision input

    Worked examples (from the shipped generator at seed 0, verified by the coordinator, §7.2): operands [100, 47], larger_first=True, expected_label="first" — direct → first, negated → first, flipped → second. Operands [199, 999], larger_first=False, expected_label="second" — direct → second, negated → second, flipped → first. The label flips with presentation order, so a position-biased candidate cannot pass all three headline arms by position alone, and the generator's larger_first alternation (slot parity, numform.py:572) keeps the direct expected-label balance exact by construction (8/8 at seed 0).

    Antonym-bundle disclosure (binding). The negated arm differs from direct in two lexical moves: insertion of the negation token ("not the") and the antonym swap (larger→smaller). Negating the direct predicate alone ("not larger") is the flipped arm. The claim in §2.4 is therefore about the antonym-negated definite description as a whole, and every headline rendering of it states the bundle. No sentence in the report may attribute a discordance to "negation" simpliciter.

    2.3 Expected-answer derivation rule (and the distinctness condition)¶

    Let the pair be (x, y), x ≠ y, presented as (first, second). Define L = the label of max(x, y), S = the label of min(x, y); L ≠ S.

    • direct: "larger" denotes max(x, y) → L.
    • negated: "the smaller" is a definite description denoting min(x, y) — it has a unique referent only because x ≠ y. "Not the smaller", read as complement within the presented pair, denotes the pair member that is not min(x, y), i.e. max(x, y) → L. The derivation is the pair-closed complement rule, not a general English equivalence. It is conditional, and the section states the condition wherever the derivation appears.
    • flipped: "smaller" denotes min(x, y) → S = the opposite label.
    • negated_alt: same derivation as negated, for its own phrasing; its scope profile (comparative + "the other" anaphora) differs slightly, which is one reason it is descriptive only.

    Distinctness condition (load-bearing, validator-enforced). If x = y, "the smaller" has no unique referent and "not the smaller" is undefined — not "either", not "both", not a third label. The generator asserts a != b (already present, numform.py:550); the diff validator independently re-derives a != b for every case and rejects the record set if any pair is equal; an equal pair is never scored, never silently dropped, and never "resolved" by a tie rule. Equal values are not a v1 population at all — a tie arm would need its own instruction ("neither" is not in the frozen answer vocabulary) and is excluded. Margin classes emitted by the generator are exactly different_length, same_length_early, same_length_last_digit (numform.py:320-342); same_length_middle is a real class the classifier reports but the generator never emits, so a record carrying it fails validation, as does equal.

    2.4 Falsifiable claim (pre-registered, one-directional)¶

    For one candidate, over the cases it completes in all three headline arms, let D be the cases it passes on the direct arm, and let J be the cases in D where, on the same case, the negated cell's decomposition is wrong_label (the smaller label — not any failure, not an echo, not a boundary miss) and the flipped cell passes. Then, per candidate:

    • D < 8 → insufficient — withheld, never defaulted to acquittal and never to a robustness claim.
    • D ≥ 8 and J ≥ 5 → negation_drop_observed — the negation-drop pattern is observed on this candidate, on this frozen phrasing, at this panel size. This is a sufficient-pattern detector, not a biconditional: it does not establish "negation-invariance" when it does not fire, and it does not diagnose every negation-related defect when it does.
    • D ≥ 8 and J < 5 → below_threshold — an observed null at this panel size, not proof of invariance. The section prints the operating characteristics in §5 beside every below_threshold.

    Named rival patterns (printed beside every decision, never hidden):

    • Larger-default / instruction-ignoring: passes direct and negated, fails flipped broadly. Visible as a low flipped pass rate; it is undiagnosed by J (J = 0 for it by construction) and must be labelled "negation-drop undiagnosed, not absent" whenever the flipped pass rate on D is below 0.5. It is not acquitted as invariant.
    • Echo-comprehension: passes direct and flipped, answers negated with a negated label form (§4.2). Visible as a high negation_echo share; it is not negation-drop and must not be reported as such.
    • Wording × position interaction: a wrong-label pattern that is concentrated in one presentation order (larger-first vs larger-second) is labelled as order-concentrated, not as general negation-drop. The section prints J split by presentation order.

    What the claim is not. No causal claim about weights, training, or "negation understanding" as a latent ability follows from any outcome. The instrument measures verdict discordance under greedy decoding on one frozen phrasing, not a mechanism. Generalisation to open-world negation is an extrapolation the report must not make: in an open-world task the complement of the negated description is not a singleton, and the expected answer is underdetermined.

    2.5 Variants: kept, descriptive, excluded (all decided)¶

    Variant Status in v1 Reason
    Negated-alt phrasing "not smaller than the other" Descriptive arm only, never a decision input, never pooled Bounds phrasing awkwardness: a joint pattern reproducing on two negated phrasings is harder to dismiss as one phrasing's artefact; a pattern appearing on only one phrasing is labelled phrasing-dependent. Its wrong-label count on D and its alt-joint count are printed descriptively. It is frozen byte-for-byte in §2.2 or it does not ship.
    Negated-flipped "not the larger" (expected = smaller) EXCLUDED from v1; named as the v2 2×2 factorial completion Fourth cell of criterion {larger, smaller} × polarity {affirmative, negated}. Its expected answer coincides with the flipped arm's, inviting pooling error, and combining two negated contrasts needs its own pre-registered rule. A v2 factorial requires a rewritten claim and fresh review, not a column added at build time.
    Negated answer option ("reply with the word that is not first") EXCLUDED Changes the answer vocabulary and the scorer's target set at once; failure could be vocabulary confusion, not negation processing.
    Imperative negation ("Do not pick the smaller…") EXCLUDED from v1 Different speech act (prohibition vs interrogative definite description) and a different scope; a genuine second probe, closer to hierarchy territory, not a variant of this one.
    Scope variants "not larger" vs "not the larger" (bare vs article) EXCLUDED as arms; REQUIRED as a design note Without the article, "not larger" in open English means ≥, which coincides with the article reading on distinct pairs and diverges on equal pairs — precisely why §2.3's distinctness assert is load-bearing. The article is not decoration; it is the scope pin.
    Double negation, affixal/implicit negation ("un-", "less", "never", "the other one") EXCLUDED from v1 Compositional and lexical negation are different mechanisms with their own ambiguity profiles. "The other one" is not a negation at all (anaphora) and must not enter a negation headline.
    Negated operand statement ("The first number is not the smaller…") EXCLUDED That is a claim arm (leading territory), not a criterion arm.

    3. Population and controls¶

    3.1 Generated cases only, stage-local, never the user Split¶

    Cases are generated by the shipped numform compare generator (same seed, same value set) and live only in the stage; they never enter the user's search/held-out Split. This is the canonical-form invariant, and it is forced here for a stronger reason than in canonical-form: variant fixtures (direct vs negated vs flipped renderings of one pair) are three fixtures about one pair, and any future Split-adjacent analysis that groups by pair would inherit a triple-counting trap. Stage-local generation avoids the question entirely, exactly as canonical-form does (canonicalform.py:17-22 in its module docstring: generated cases only, stage-local, never the Split). Note the Split mechanics for the record: Split compares message inputs by NFC key and rejects identical message inputs across the split (core.py:169, class Split; uniqueness at line 204, identical-message rejection at line 214) — variant fixtures are not identical messages, so they would not collide, but the triple-counting trap above is the reason to stay stage-local regardless.

    3.2 Value ranges, strata, form, and presentation¶

    • Values: reuse numform's bands verbatim — numform.py:99: BANDS = ((1, 3), (4, 6), (7, 9), (10, 12)) digit lengths, 4 values per band per task, giving 16 compare cases per candidate. Margin strata cycle by slot — different_length, same_length_early, same_length_last_digit — and the headline is reported per stratum descriptively, never pooled across strata into a single "negation rate" that hides a stratum effect (e.g. a last-digit-only effect would be a digit-discrimination story, not a negation story). Seed-0 shape (verifier-confirmed): strata 8/4/4, bands 3/4/5/4, 8/16 length-decidable (different_length — the larger is necessarily the longer string, so length alone decides those cases).
    • Form: plain only. Crossing negation with form would be a 3 × 4 (4 × 4 with the descriptive arm) panel whose headline could not attribute a flip to either axis — the payload F3 bundle again. Plain is the form where the operands are bare digit strings and the only moving part is the instruction, which is the point. Form-crossing is a v2 panel with its own claim.
    • Presentation order: the shipped larger_first alternation (numform.py:572-573) is kept byte-for-byte, so direct expected labels are balanced first/second by construction and no new order bias is introduced.
    • Answer contract and decoding: byte-identical reply contract in every arm (§2.2); greedy decoding, same --max-new-tokens, same boundary ids, same seed, same template family per candidate. Templates are not touched: negation lives inside the user message content only.
    • Closed-book screen: none, and canonical-form is the precedent. Placement's screen (placement.py:370-381, screened(): a record is screened when its closed-book cell completed and passed) does not transfer, for a structural reason: in placement the payload is a prose fact passage that can be removed while leaving a well-formed question; in compare, the operands are the task content, and removing them leaves a question with no ground truth. The screen's function — excluding cases the treatment cannot move — is already served by conditioning on D: a case the candidate fails directly is a case negation-drop cannot be shown to move, and it is excluded from the headline population by construction. Canonical-form explicitly takes the same position (canonicalform.py:556-557: "There is no closed-book screen in this probe").

    3.3 Counts (frozen, with derivations)¶

    • Headline generations: 16 cases × 3 arms × 24 candidates (run_experiment's cap, per the placement comment on its 24-candidate panel) = 1,152.
    • With the descriptive alt arm: 16 × 4 × 24 = 1,536 generations.
    • Ground records: 24 candidates × 16 cases = 384 records, in the canonical multi-cell shape (one record per candidate × case, one cell per arm — the canonical-form record shape, not numform's one-record-per-generation shape; this choice is what makes the arithmetic above the record arithmetic, and the diff validator regenerates arms from the same shape).
    • The build cycle states the byte/record cap derivation in CLI help from this shape (the sibling pattern: a frozen cap constant with its derivation, enforced by the stage and re-derived by the validator), and prints these numbers with derivations, not as bare constants.

    3.4 Token-ID divergence: reported, not gated¶

    Canonical-form's headline population is ID-divergent paired cases only (canonicalform.py:399, _pair_block_divergent), because NFC/NFD renderings can collapse to identical id sequences, in which case the instrument is inapplicable rather than the model robust. For negation the situation is inverted: the arms differ in instruction text by construction, so their full render id sequences will diverge on any ordinary tokenizer, and divergence-gating would be vacuous — it would gate on a condition that is true by construction and hide the one informative event, a same-ids case, which indicates a tokenizer/rendering pathology. (The research brief's universal "will diverge on any sane tokenizer" is REMOVED: it is an unexecuted universal. The design reports divergence and treats same-ids as an anomaly, which covers both outcomes without asserting either.)

    Required machinery (canonical-form shape, different gating):

    • Per case, per arm, record the full render id sequence digest and length, following the canonical-form stage pattern (experiments.py:9639-9647: ids_identical and id_length_delta computed from the arms' render ids at construction).
    • Report, per candidate: n_ids_identical across arm pairs (expected 0), the per-case id_length_delta list in case order for direct-vs-negated (expected positive: the negated instruction is longer), and max_abs_id_delta.
    • Headline population = cases completed in all three headline arms, not id-divergent. A same-ids case, if one ever occurs, is excluded from the headline population and counted separately as a rendering anomaly, never as a pass or a fail, and a same-ids verdict flip is recorded as a determinism finding, never as negation fragility (payload-round rule).
    • Token-count delta is NOT reported as a measured quantity (see F3): the tree has no frozen local tokenizer. Character delta and render-id length delta are the reported quantities.

    4. Scorer pins and fail-closed vocabulary (binding)¶

    4.1 Headline scorer¶

    core.evaluate's byte-exact verdict (core.py:797: exact_match = status == "completed" and answer == case.expected), conjoined with boundary presence in the stage cell (the canonical-form cell pattern: cell verdict = exact_match and boundary_found). No stripping, no parsing, no normalisation is added to the headline path. The scorer-lens ladder (scorerlens.py:137, RUNGS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)) may be reported as a sensitivity beside the headline — in particular, a negated-arm failure that recovers at R1/R3 (whitespace/fence) is a scorer artefact, not a negation discordance — but it never rescues a flip into a pass, and the ladder has no label-strip operation, so "Answer: first" does not recover against "first" at any rung.

    4.2 Decomposition and the frozen negation_echo subclass¶

    Every completed cell is decomposed by numform's cell_decomposition (numform.py:840-863) into exact_pass / wrong_label / non_label, where for compare wrong_label means the answer digest equals the digest of the other label and non_label means anything else. On top of that, the build defines and the validator mirrors exactly one frozen subclass:

    • negation_echo: a completed cell whose raw answer is, exactly (byte-for-byte, no stripping, no casefolding), one of the negated forms of the other label for that arm — that is, for an arm whose expected label is E with other label O, the answer is "not " + O or "not the " + O. Concretely: expected first → echo answers "not second", "not the second"; expected second → echo answers "not first", "not the first". Any other negated form ("not the second one", "not second.", "NOT second") is non_label (other), not echo — the set is frozen, and enlarging it at build time voids this verdict.

    Reporting rules: per arm, the section prints the exact_pass / wrong_label / negation_echo / non_label (other) split. The negation_echo share is printed beside every negated-arm rate, never pooled into wrong_label and never into a pass. The joint rule (§2.4) counts only wrong_label, so echoes cannot fire it; a candidate whose negated failures are predominantly echo is reported as echo-dominant, not as negation-drop, and the section must say so in words, not merely in a table column.

    • Boundary decomposition (mandatory): every discordance is decomposed into exact-only vs boundary-only. A boundary-only discordance (answer text equals expected, boundary missed) is a boundary effect, never a negation effect, and cannot count toward J. The cell already carries boundary_found, so this decomposition costs no new machinery — but it must be printed, not merely available.
    • Expected values are the two ASCII labels first / second (canonical-form SCORER PIN transplant: decomposable characters live in carrier prose only; here there is no carrier prose at all, so the pin is satisfied trivially and the scorer cannot be a form/encoding confound).
    • Per-arm attrition (n_context_exceeded, n_evaluation_failed) is printed beside every paired rate; ungated discordance is printed as a sensitivity wherever any gating applies.

    4.3 Fail-closed vocabulary (frozen before building)¶

    Following the sibling pattern (e.g. density's stage details ("cancelled", "density_record_budget_exceeded", "density_screen_failed") at density.py:131-136, and per-record details ("", "arm_evaluation_failed", "arm_context_exceeded")):

    • Stage details exactly: {cancelled, negation_record_budget_exceeded, negation_screen_failed}. negation_screen_failed is bound to one real screen: the pre-generation audit screen over the frozen instruction strings and the generated set — special-token-literal audit over every frozen instruction string for every family (the fixtures.py:471 audit_special_token_literals pattern), distinctness re-derivation (a != b for every case), and margin-class validity (only the three emitted classes). Failure of that screen makes the stage unavailable with that detail. (Red-team A's negation_template_screen_failed was considered and not adopted: the screen covers the whole generated set, not the template alone.)
    • Per-cell details exactly: {"", arm_context_exceeded, arm_evaluation_failed}.
    • Whole-stage failure: any generator failure, renderer failure, serializer exception, or non-UTF-8 payload degrades the whole stage to {"available": False, "detail": "negation_unavailable"} — never to a per-cell verdict. A validator rejection of the record set (including an equal pair) is likewise whole-stage negation_unavailable, not a per-cell drop.
    • Model parse failure is scored wrong, never a detail. No new verdict words. In particular: no arm_parse_failed, no per-arm "not applicable", no negation_too_large.
    • Section decision labels (negation_drop_observed, below_threshold, insufficient) are section labels only, not cell or stage details, and are defined solely by §2.4.

    4.4 Pre-generation audit (counts, never silent drops)¶

    Cases failing the §4.3 audit screen are excluded pre-generation with counts (n_audit_excluded, by reason), never silently dropped. For the shipped generator the expected audit-excluded count is 0 (distinctness is enforced by construction); a nonzero count is itself a finding about the generator and must be printed, not normalised away. Screened cases are never scored.

    5. Report shape and operating characteristics (printed, not footnoted)¶

    Per candidate, the section prints, in this order:

    1. Per-arm pass rates (direct / negated / flipped / negated_alt descriptive) with per-arm attrition beside each.
    2. The decomposition split per arm (§4.2), with the negation_echo share separated on the negated arms.
    3. D, J, and the decision (§2.4), with J split by presentation order (larger-first / larger-second) and by stratum.
    4. The rival-pattern readouts: flipped pass rate on D (with the gate-weak qualifier when below 0.5), echo-dominant flag, and order-concentration flag.
    5. id_length_delta list (direct-vs-negated, case order) and max_abs_id_delta; n_ids_identical (expected 0; any nonzero count is a determinism finding, §3.4).
    6. Boundary-only vs exact-only discordance decomposition.
    7. The operating-characteristics paragraph, verbatim in substance:

    "At D = N, this panel (16 cases) falsifies only negation-drop patterns at least as lopsided as J ≥ 5 at D ≥ 8. It does not establish invariance. Measured operating characteristics of the (8,5) rule: a random labeller (independent 0.5 labels per arm) fires ~4% of the time; a competent candidate with no negation effect (direct/flipped pass 0.9, negated wrong-label rate 0.1) fires 0.7%; power is 84% at a 0.5 drop rate (direct/flipped pass 0.9) and 99%+ at 0.75, but only ~21% at a 0.25 drop rate. Small effects are therefore usually missed; a below_threshold on this panel is weak evidence."

    Records carry digests only (per-arm content digests, token counts, ids_identical, id_length_delta, cells, details — canonical-form record shape). Reports render counts/rates only, no payload/question/answer text, every payload-derived string through _escape_text (reporting.py:28), never bare escape().

    No winner headline, no pooled discordance. Discordance is never pooled across candidates/templates: per-candidate contrasts only. The per-case joint classification is the primary readout; per-arm rates are descriptive context beside it.

    6. Scope justification (red-team A M3, required section)¶

    Surface-noise deliberately excludes semantic instruction variation: its not_measured list includes "semantic paraphrase of instructions" (surfacenoise.py, listed twice in the module's scope declarations), and its perturbation axis is surface-only. This probe re-enters that scope for exactly one variation, on three grounds that paraphrase generally does not meet:

    1. Derivable ground truth. The negated arm's expected answer is derivable by construction from the pair (pair-closed complement, §2.3), without labelling, judging, or a gold set. General paraphrase has no such derivation: a paraphrase's expected answer is "the same as the original" by stipulation, which is exactly the payload round's F2 defect (a false "same content" claim).
    2. One frozen treatment, not a family. The variation is one frozen string (§2.2), not "paraphrase" as an open class. Its scope is the definite-description mechanism, stated in the claim (§2.4), not "instruction meaning" in general.
    3. A sibling precedent for meaning-bearing instruction variation with controls. Leading varies a stated claim and hierarchy varies instruction privilege, both meaning-bearing, both with joint per-case disciplines — the lab's scope exclusion is specific to surface-noise's axis, not a lab-wide ban on semantic treatments. What surface-noise excludes is uncontrolled semantic variation; this probe's variation is controlled by construction (§2.3, §3, §4).

    If the build cycle widens the variation beyond the frozen string (any second negated phrasing as a decision input, any paraphrase arm, any imperative arm), it exits this justification and voids this verdict.
