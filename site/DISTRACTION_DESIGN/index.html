    Distractor dilution probe — design¶

    model-protocol-lab run --distract answers a deployment question the selection loop cannot see: once a template is chosen, how much irrelevant context can be poured around the real prompt before the chosen candidate starts failing — and does irrelevant text that merely looks like it contains a different answer actively capture the model, or does failure under load come from dilution alone? Retrieval pipelines, long system preambles, and multi-document prompts all add exactly this kind of load, so the answer decides how much context budget a deployment can spend before the protocol itself becomes the risk.

    The probe is post-hoc and observational: it reuses the recorded search observations as the native (dose 0) arm, generates treated cells on the same backend, and never touches candidate selection. The primary decision is reported per condition only as a flip marker against the recorded outcome.

    Conditions and protocol¶

    Ten conditions, frozen in code (distraction.CONDITIONS):

    • neutral filler at 64, 256, and 1024 tokens, placed before and after the fixture's last user message (6 cells per fixture);
    • near-miss filler at 64 and 256 tokens, before and after (4 cells per fixture).

    Near-miss cells exist only where a planted answer is defined (see below); at 1024 the near-miss arm does not exist, so capture curves terminate at 256. That is a scope decision, not an omission: the near-miss claim is about capture at moderate load, and the generation budget (below) is spent on the neutral dose curve first.

    Filler is whole corpus sentences joined by a single newline, nested by construction — the dose-d span's sentence sequence is a prefix of the dose-1024 sequence — with the final sentence truncated to hit the exact token target where whole sentences cannot. The sizing rule (whole-sentences-plus-one-truncated-suffix-v1) searches candidate spans by rendered token delta against the native render and takes the first exact hit within a frozen encode budget (512 encodes per cell); a cell that cannot be sized exactly is recorded arm_unmatchable, never approximated. The rendered dose is an identity the diff validator re-derives for every compared cell: cell.input_tokens - native.input_tokens must equal the nominal level.

    Both corpora are frozen constants whose SHA-256 digests ride in the protocol block: a 71-sentence neutral corpus (no digits, no special-token literals) and four near-miss sentence templates with a single {planted} slot, cycled to fill the span. Every span is screened before generation: special-token literals for any candidate, or the candidate's own boundary token texts appearing in the span, reject the cell as arm_screened — filler must never smuggle protocol structure into the prompt.

    The planted answer (digit-string surgery v2)¶

    A fixture's expected answer yields a planted string only when it is a plain ASCII number: optional ASCII sign, an integer part either plain or comma-grouped in threes (or a bare fraction like .5), an optional fraction, an optional exponent. The plant is the same string with the last mantissa digit incremented, carry propagating through digits only, regrouping commas on overflow ("9,999" → "10,000", "007" → "008", "-0" → "-1", "42.0" → "42.1", "1,000.5" → "1,000.6"). Everything else — surrounding whitespace, underscores, non-ASCII digits, inf/NaN, dates, number words — yields no plant. The rule is total and deterministic, independent of any ambient decimal context; the exact battery lives in the cycle-184 tests. Where no plant exists, or the plant equals another fixture's expected answer, or the candidate's native answer already is the plant, the near-miss arm is undefined for that (candidate, fixture) and its cells record near_miss_undefined.

    Scope, stated plainly: near-miss capture is defined only for fixtures whose expected answer is a number in the ASCII shape above. On answer sets without such fixtures the near-miss arm is empty by construction and the probe reduces to the neutral dose curves.

    Claims and denominators¶

    • D1′ (dose curve). Per candidate, per filler kind and placement, the paired pass rate at each level: records where the native arm passes or fails, the cell was generated, and both sides exist. Rates are reported per dose, never pooled across placements or kinds.
    • D2′ (first flip). Over native-passing records with a complete curve, the distribution of the first failing level, plus two counters the flip histogram alone would hide: returns (passing levels above the first flip — recovery under more load is evidence the flip was noise, not a threshold) and gains (native-failing records that pass at some dose).
    • D3′ (capture, an upper bound). Over native-passing records whose near-miss cell was generated, the share whose answer is exactly the planted string. Byte-exact scoring can only undercount capture (a captured answer with stray formatting scores as a plain failure), so the rate is labelled an upper bound on clean capture in the report. The headline number is the excess over neutral: records captured under near-miss whose same-dose neutral answer is not the plant. Dilution alone cannot manufacture the planted string in the neutral arm, so the excess separates active capture from generic load failure.
    • D4′ (per-condition selection). The conservative selection rule re-run per condition over the candidates that completed that condition on the same fixture set. Where completed sets differ, selection is withheld for that condition (selection_withheld_mismatched_sets) rather than compared across different evidence. Flips are marked against the recorded primary decision under the same vocabulary mapping the diff validator uses.
    • D5′ (construction honesty). Every cell records how its treated prompt relates to the native render: id_exact when the token ids decompose exactly into native prefix + inserted span + native suffix (the intended surgery), text_level when only the text decomposes (a tokenizer seam merged across the junction). Seam token ids (first/last inserted id) are recorded for text-level cells. A dose claim is only as strong as its construction, and the report keeps the two apart.

    Vacuous fixtures (empty expected answer, or expected contained in the prompt) and echo fixtures are counted and excluded from every rate. Records whose native arm is unavailable make the whole candidate unavailable: the probe never mixes a partial native arm with generated cells.

    Cost¶

    Worst case is frozen in the protocol: 10 conditions × 2,400 ground records = 24,000 generations, where ground records are candidate × fixture pairs with a completed native arm. The CLI help states the count and the local backend's forwards formula (M·L + M(M−1)/2 for M forwards of growing length L with the KV cache off) so the price is visible before the run, not after. Per-cell sizing probes are capped separately (512 encodes) and never generate.

    What the probe does not do¶

    • No grouping readout in v1: the think/non-think style grouping used by the repetition probe's disclosure is not repeated here; construction class and capture carry the honesty load.
    • No capture claim at 1024 (see Conditions).
    • No cross-backend claims: the protocol fingerprint binds the backend fingerprint, both corpus digests, the rule versions, the budgets, and the fixture set; a diff across differing protocols reports drift and refuses comparability.
    • The diff validator recomputes every derived value from the records (curves, first-flip state machine, capture, per-condition selection, fingerprint) and rejects payloads whose shape pre-checks fail (record caps, byte bound, digest shapes) before recomputing. Like every recompute validator in this project, it proves internal consistency of the payload against the frozen protocol — it cannot prove the generations really happened; that is what the sealed bundle and the determinism audit are for.
