    Schema field-order probe — design (cycle 205, wave-9 rank 4)¶

    Status: v2, adjudicated after the adversarial round and shipped in cycle 205 (agents_out/cycle205/{redteam_a,redteam_b,verifier, coordinator_adjudication}.md: A filed 2 FATAL / 9 MAJOR / 4 MINOR, the verifier reproduced every load-bearing number, B confirmed both FATALs with corrections and added NB1–NB6). v1's defects that shaped this rewrite: answer labels the contexts do not entail (F1), a self-contradictory non-finite sentinel with an undefined canonical form (F2), overstated arm distinctness (F3), unfrozen new specs (F4), and a decision rule whose printed operating characteristics understated its weakness (F5/F6).

    Utility¶

    A practitioner defining a JSON schema for extraction learns, per template family, whether putting the main answer field first vs last in the schema changes the values the model produces for it — before a cosmetic schema reorder in a code review silently changes extraction accuracy in production.

    Question and claim¶

    For a fixed candidate (chat template) and a fixed set of flat JSON extraction/classification/routing specs, is the value produced for a spec's designated answer field invariant under the field's position in the presented schema?

    Pre-registered claim: per-field value accuracy is invariant under schema field order. Proved wrong, per candidate, if the designated field's paired accuracy differs between the answer-first and answer-last arms on specs adherent in both arms, under the decision rule below. Intermediate arms (canonical, reversed) are descriptive context, never decision inputs.

    Motivation, stated precisely: Tam et al. (arXiv:2408.02442) establish that format restrictions degrade reasoning; their abstract makes no key-order claim. Their §4.1 does report direct key-order evidence of a different kind: 100% of GPT-3.5 JSON-mode Last-Letter responses placed the "answer" key before the "reason" key, "resulting in zero-shot direct answering", and the authors conclude that "the order of keys in structured outputs … emerge as important factors". That is emitted key order under constrained JSON mode, on a reasoning task — not the order in which a prompt presents a schema's fields. The presentation-order hypothesis — a field the schema presents earlier is also emitted earlier, with less of the object's content in context — is the lab's own reasoning, built on Tam et al.'s two verified results. JSONSchemaBench (arXiv:2501.10868, cited by structured.py) varies constrained-decoding frameworks and models, not prompt-only field order crossed with chat templates. The combination — prompt-only field order × template family × per-field exact scoring — is the contribution; no "first" is claimed.

    Spec set¶

    The shipped structured spec set (9 specs) is reused unchanged, and six further specs in the same flat dialect are added in fieldorder.py (the structured set itself is not modified). Reason for the addition, stated plainly: the shipped schemas have at most 2 fields, and three have exactly 1, so on the shipped set alone the arms collapse to at most two distinct orders per spec and the reversed arm is rarely a distinct treatment. The new specs carry 3–4 fields.

    Canonical order is the order the shipped instrument presents: alphabetical by field name (structured.describe_schema sorts properties and the required list). Each spec designates one answer field and a frozen paired flag. Pairing rule (derived, from frozen data): a spec enters a candidate's paired population iff paired is yes, its first/last orders differ, both endpoint records are adherent, and neither endpoint record has duplicate keys.

    Reader-entailment rule (F1): a spec may be paired: yes only if a reader given the question, context, and schema — but not the answer table — can derive the answer field's value uniquely. Two shipped specs fail it and are paired: no (kept as adherence/descriptive specs; the shipped set cannot be edited): route-alert (channel = pager — nothing maps 97% disk usage to pager over sms/email) and route-order (warehouse = south — nothing entails it; Porto is in northern Portugal). Non-answer fields of shipped specs may carry frozen judgement values (route-ticket's priority = 3); they feed descriptive per-field readouts only and are named here so the noise is not discovered later.

    The six new specs, frozen in full (schema dialect exactly as in structured.py; every answer passes check_schema against its own schema, verified at module load in tests):

    • extract-product (extraction, paired yes). Q: "Extract the product's brand, model, price and stock status." Context: "The catalogue lists it as: brand Acme, model Rotor X2. It costs 349 dollars and is currently in stock." Schema: brand (string), model (string), price (integer ≥ 0), in_stock (boolean). Answer: {brand "Acme", model "Rotor X2", price 349, in_stock true}. Answer field: model. (The catalogue split is stated verbatim in the context, so the answer field is determined, not convention-dependent.)
    • extract-event (extraction, paired yes). Q: "Extract the event venue, day and attendance." Context: "The workshop meets at Harbor Hall on Friday with 40 attendees." Schema: venue (string), day (string, enum monday…sunday, all lowercase), attendance (integer ≥ 0). Answer: {venue "Harbor Hall", day "friday", attendance 40}. Answer field: venue.
    • route-complaint (routing, paired yes). Q: "Route the complaint and record whether a defect was confirmed and whether data was lost." Context: "Complaint: 'The update deleted my saved files.' Front-line support confirmed the update itself is defective." Schema: team (string, enum [sales, support, technical]), defect_confirmed (boolean), data_lost (boolean). Answer: {team "technical", defect_confirmed true, data_lost true}. Answer field: team. (Every field is entailed: files deleted, defect confirmed by support, and a confirmed product defect routes past front-line support to technical.)
    • classify-document (classification, paired yes). Q: "Classify the document's type, language and confidentiality." Context: "Document: a German-language internal memo marked confidential." Schema: doc_type (string, enum [memo, report, invoice]), language (string, enum [english, german, french]), confidential (boolean). Answer: {doc_type "memo", language "german", confidential true}. Answer field: doc_type.
    • extract-vehicle (extraction, paired yes). Q: "Extract the vehicle make, year, fuel and seat count." Context: "The 2019 Volvo wagon runs on diesel and seats five." Schema: make (string), year (integer 1900–2100), fuel (string, enum [petrol, diesel, electric]), seats (integer ≥ 1). Answer: {make "Volvo", year 2019, fuel "diesel", seats 5}. Answer field: make.
    • route-delivery (routing, paired yes). Q: "Route the delivery and flag its requirements." Context: "Delivery: vaccines to a clinic in the south, sent by express courier for tomorrow morning; a signature is required and the box must stay refrigerated." Schema: region (string, enum [north, south, east, west]), speed (string, enum [standard, express]), signature_required (boolean), refrigerated (boolean). Answer: {region "south", speed "express", signature_required true, refrigerated true}. Answer field: region.

    Paired pool: 10 specs (extraction 6, routing 3, classification 1). Across them the answer field sits first in 3 canonical orders, last in 3, and in a middle position in 4, so the endpoint contrast is not confounded with "the answer field is always the alphabetically-first field".

    Arms¶

    Per spec, four renderings of the same prompt, differing only in the field enumeration order inside the schema description (the property list and the required list both follow the arm order):

    • canonical: alphabetical (the shipped presentation).
    • answer_first: the answer field moved to the front; the remaining fields keep canonical relative order.
    • answer_last: the answer field moved to the end; the remaining fields keep canonical relative order.
    • reversed: canonical order reversed (generic-reordering control).

    Distinctness, stated exactly (F3): the four orders are all distinct only when the answer field sits in a middle position (4 specs). When it sits at a canonical endpoint with ≥ 3 fields, there are 3 distinct orders (canonical coincides with that endpoint arm; 2 specs). With 2 fields there are 2 distinct orders (6 specs); with 1 field, 1 (3 specs). Canonical therefore coincides with an endpoint arm on 6 of the 10 paired specs, and its readouts there are replicates of that endpoint arm under greedy decoding — labelled as such wherever canonical numbers are printed next to endpoint numbers. Per candidate the stage runs 60 arm-generations covering 37 distinct prompts; the duplicate generations are kept (uniform code path and record shape), and the cost is printed as 60, not 37. The decision is unaffected: answer-first ≠ answer-last on all 10 paired specs.

    The prompt template is structured.render_prompt's, with the schema description rendered in the arm's order by an order-parametrised describe function whose canonical output is byte-identical to structured.describe_schema (pinned by a test). Shipped specs keep the (ref: structured/<id>) marker; new specs use (ref: fieldorder/<id>). The schema presentation and the implied output key order move together — that is the production change being tested; no separate output-order instruction exists in the shipped prompt, and none is added.

    Records and recompute (residue stated, §Records)¶

    Generation mirrors the density/rotation stages: one record per (candidate, spec, arm), exactly 15 × 4 = 60 per candidate in canonical (spec, arm) order with unique (spec, arm) keys — the validator rejects any other record set for a candidate (NB1). Records come from core.evaluate at MAX_NEW_TOKENS = 64, fixed by the protocol rather than threaded from the CLI: the record caps below are derived from it (NB4). Budget cap: 24 candidates → MAX_FIELD_ORDER_RECORDS = 1,440. Screening mirrors density: the stage-level special-token audit runs over the frozen text inventory (questions, contexts, templates, description components), and a per-spec boundary-containment screen runs over the rendered prompts (NB5). Stage details {cancelled, field_order_record_budget_exceeded, field_order_screen_failed}; per-record detail ∈ {"", arm_evaluation_failed, arm_context_exceeded}.

    Record shape (frozen):

    {candidate, spec_id, arm, detail, case_screened, boundary_found,
 duplicate_keys, answer_digest, document_kind, document_digest,
 field_values, input_tokens, content_digest}

    Digest preimages (NB2): content_digest = SHA-256 of the arm's rendered user content (the prompt string), recomputed by the validator from its literal spec table. answer_digest = SHA-256 of the decoded answer text, or None when there was no generation.

    • document_kind: "object" | "non_object" | "unparseable" | "" ("" exactly when there was no generation). unparseable means JSON parse failure only.
    • duplicate_keys: a stage-observed bool — the raw answer contained a repeated object key. Scoring consequences (derived, below): a duplicate-key document is never adherent and never enters pairing, because its extraction is parser-dependent (Python's json.loads keeps the last occurrence; a production parser may keep the first). Per-field readouts score the last-wins parse, with duplicate counts printed beside them.
    • field_values: for "object" documents, a dict mapping every produced key to the canonical JSON string of its parsed value (canonical form pinned below), or to None when the produced value has no canonical form — a non-finite number (NaN/±Infinity, which json.loads accepts as a Python extension) or a value beyond the int↔str digit limit. A produced JSON null is the string "null", never None — the sentinel is unambiguous. {} for every other kind, and for degraded records (detail set or screened: kind "", digest None, field_values {}).
    • document_digest: the digest of the document's canonical form, stored only where the validator recomputes it: non-None ⟺ kind is "object" and no field_values entry is None; then it must equal the digest of the reconstructed document. Otherwise None.

    Canonical JSON, pinned (F2c): structured._canonical_json exactly — json.dumps(value, sort_keys=True, separators=(", ", ": "), ensure_ascii=True, allow_nan=False). Worked example: the document {"name": "Ada Lovelace", "age": 36} canonicalises to the string {"age": 36, "name": "Ada Lovelace"} (note the spaced separators — this probe's canonical form is the structured convention, not density's compact one).

    Everything scored is derived from the record fields, by the producer's aggregator and the diff validator's mirror alike:

    • A field's produced value is correct iff its key is present in field_values with a string whose digest equals the expected value's canonical digest (expected values come from the frozen spec table). A None (non-finite) value is incorrect for every field, since no expected value is a non-finite number. Correctness is exact canonical equality; every numeric field in the spec set is integer (F11), and number-typed fields are out of scope for answer-field designation.
    • The schema verdict is recomputed by re-running the flat schema check over the reconstructed document (each field_values string parsed back; the validator requires every stored string to be exactly the canonical form of its parse, and requires document_digest consistency as above — so the stored values are bound, not attested). A None value fails its key's field check before reconstruction, so a null-typed field can never be passed by the sentinel (no current spec has a null field; the rule is frozen anyway). "non_object" and "unparseable" documents are schema-failed by kind.
    • Validator totality (F10): any exception while parsing or canonicalising a stored string — ValueError (including the int↔str digit limit), RecursionError, TypeError — makes the section invalid, never a crash. Both mirrors use a safe canonicaliser with that catch (classify_response's RecursionError handling is the in-tree precedent).
    • Adherence per record: parse_ok iff kind ∈ {object, non_object}; adherent iff parse_ok, the recomputed schema verdict is ok, and not duplicate_keys — structured's parse/schema decomposition, kept, so a value effect cannot hide inside an adherence effect.

    Caps (F8, derived from enforced premises): per record, the summed length of field_values strings is at most 4,096 chars, validator-enforced. Derivation: MAX_NEW_TOKENS = 64 and at most 32 decoded chars per generated token for the tokenizers this lab targets bound an answer at 2,048 chars; the cap is 2× that. Producer rule: a live record whose values exceed the cap is stored with detail arm_evaluation_failed and empty field_values (counted under n_detail_screened). Payload cap MAX_FIELD_ORDER_PAYLOAD_BYTES = 8 MiB: 1,440 records × (4,096 + ≈ 1.4 KB of other fields and JSON overhead) ≈ 7.9 MB.

    Stated residue (F9): the link between answer_digest and (document_kind, field_values) is stage-attested — the validator recomputes every verdict from the record fields, but it cannot prove the fields came from the answer the digest names (the same origin-only residue density discloses). duplicate_keys and boundary_found are stage-observed facts of the same status; every consequence drawn from them is recomputed.

    Aggregation and decision¶

    The aggregate candidate block always carries detail ("" or "field_order_unavailable") plus every readout key below, so a zero-live candidate prints None rates and an insufficient decision with n_paired = 0 rather than vanishing (NB6). Live = not screened, no detail, boundary found.

    • Adherence by arm: n, parse/schema/adherent rates over live records (structured's decomposition; rates None iff n = 0).
    • Per-field accuracy by arm: for every field of every spec, correct / live-in-arm (missing counts incorrect; denominators printed). Plus whole-document accuracy by arm — all spec fields correct and no extra keys in field_values — over live records (F15).
    • Answer-field accuracy by arm over all live records, and over the paired population below.
    • Pairing and attrition: per candidate, the paired population per the rule above; attrition printed per arm (specs lost to non-adherence, to duplicate keys, to the paired flag / order rule). Beside the paired counts, the ungated discordant counts — all paired: yes specs whose endpoint orders differ, scoring non-adherent records' answer fields by their field_values — are printed as a sensitivity, because adherence gating is outcome-dependent selection: a generation failure that makes the answer field wrong in one arm often makes that record non-adherent and removes the spec from pairing, deflating measured discordance (F6; the deflation is bounded in this instrument — most incorrect values here are adherent-shaped, e.g. a wrong enum member — but the direction is disclosed, not hidden).
    • Decision: per paired spec, answer-field correct in first / correct in last; discordant counts n_first_only, n_last_only; exact two-sided sign test over discordant specs. falsified iff discordant ≥ MIN_DISCORDANT_SPECS = 6 and p ≤ 0.05 (direction recorded); insufficient iff discordant < 6 — the boolean is withheld, never defaulted to acquittal; else not_falsified. Printed sensitivities: the same decision recomputed on the extraction-only paired specs (6), and the paired counts split by field count (2-field vs ≥ 3-field — on 2-field specs the answer field's promotion is the companion's demotion, F13).
    • Operating characteristics, printed wherever the verdict is (F5/F12): critical splits — at 6–8 discordant specs only a unanimous split falsifies; at 9, 8–1 (p = 0.0391); at 10, 9–1 (p = 0.0215). Power: a true 0.90-vs-0.60 endpoint accuracy gap yields falsified only ≈ 15% of the time (simulated, independent specs); most such runs print insufficient or not_falsified. The sign test's α is exact under independent discordance directions across specs; specs are a fixed panel, not a sample, and within-shape direction correlation degrades the calibration (at the extreme of one direction coin per shape, conditioned on full discordance, the false-falsification rate reaches ≈ 50%; unconditional rates stayed ≤ ≈ 4.4% across the round's parameter sweep). Per-candidate α = 0.05 gives ≈ 1.2 expected false falsifications across 24 candidates at the nominal rate (probability of at least one ≈ 0.71); the realised per-candidate rate is below nominal by discreteness.

    Protocol object: {seed, backend fingerprint, spec/arm inventory, and digests of three frozen rule texts — GENERATION (spec set, designations, paired flags, arm derivation), RENDERING (prompt construction), CHECKING (canonicalisation, sentinel, duplicate rule, correctness, schema recompute, pairing, decision)} — mirror-recomputed by the validator from its own literal copies, as in cycles 203/204.

    Known limits (printed in the report)¶

    • Flat schemas of 1–4 fields; endpoint placement only — nothing is measured about middle positions as a treatment.
    • Canonical coincides with an endpoint arm on 6 of 10 paired specs; single-field specs cannot enter the pairing; the reversed arm coincides with an endpoint on 2-field specs.
    • Two shipped specs are excluded from pairing because their frozen answer labels are not entailed by their contexts (route-alert, route-order); one shipped non-answer field is a frozen judgement value (route-ticket priority).
    • Adherence gating selects specs the candidate already handles, and selects against discordance; the paired claim is conditional on adherence in both endpoint arms, with the ungated counts printed as sensitivity.
    • Duplicate-key documents are scored by their last occurrence in per-field readouts and excluded from adherence/pairing.
    • 10 paired specs and a sign test: only very large endpoint effects can falsify, and they usually will not (≈ 15% power at a 30-point gap). The verdict's printed characteristics say so.
