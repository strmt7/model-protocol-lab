    Choice-scoring audit design (run --choice-audit, cycle 195)¶

    Status: frozen contract, red-team cross-checked before code (research wave-8 rank #6). The swarm record is in docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md (cycle 195): researcher brief, red-team A (16 findings, verdict BUILD WITH AMENDMENTS), red-team B (every executed claim of A's re-run bit-identically; 6 findings confirmed, 10 partially confirmed, 9 sub-claims refuted, 5 B-NEW findings). This document is the contract the build implements; where the original wave-8 brief and this document disagree, this document governs.

    1. Utility¶

    Monday morning: a practitioner deciding whether to trust the lab's greedy-generation winner — or an lm-eval-style log-likelihood ranking they got elsewhere — learns, per template family, how often teacher-forced choice scoring and free generation pick different answers on the same fixtures, and whether the two methods' top candidates agree. The audit is a second opinion: it never selects, never feeds the selection rule, and its payload contains no decision about the run. (Vocabulary rule in §10.)

    2. What is measured¶

    Post-hoc over a completed run, teacher-forced only. The audit adds no generations beyond the native search it audits; its cost is forward passes (§9). For each search fixture, a four-choice set is constructed from the run's own search fixtures (§3). Each choice is teacher-forced under each candidate over that candidate's recorded search prompt for the fixture, using the existing scoring.py engine. Three rankings of the same cells are reported (§4). Headlines per candidate: choice accuracy under the total ranking, the generation × choice discordance table (§7), and difficulty strata (§7). Method level: whether the choice method's top candidate agrees with the run's primary winner, computed only over the common evaluable population (§6).

    Two scope sentences that ship in the docs and CLI help with the feature:

    • The generation side is the lab's greedy generation. Nothing here speaks to sampled decoding.
    • The choices are scored in separate forward passes over the same recorded prompt; they are never presented to the model as a list. There is no position or primacy confound and no permutation arm.

    3. Choice-set construction (frozen order of operations)¶

    Construction is a pure function of the search split. The order is frozen (red-team B, B-NEW-5): construct → screen → dedup → score → non-finite demotion → evaluability → argmax and tie classification, per ranking. No step may be reordered by an implementer; the producer and the diff validator walk the same order.

    1. Exclusions. A fixture whose expected answer normalises to empty at rung 1 of the scorer-lens ladder is excluded from every choice population, as a target and as a donor, with the exclusion counted (scorer-lens / leading precedent; rung 1 begins with a trim, so strip-empty is the same set — one exclusion, one reason, blank_expected). A blank true choice would score ok_empty with total 0.0 — the maximum attainable — and win its set by construction; that is a fixture defect, not a measurement.
    2. Donor rule smallest-eligible-id-first-three-v1 (probe-local name; modelled on leading's donor rule, extended from one donor to three — leading's machinery itself yields exactly one plant per fixture and is not reused). For target fixture T, the donor pool is every non-excluded search fixture whose group differs from T's group (donors may share a group among themselves). A pool fixture is eligible when its expected is non-equivalent to every expected in T's group under the screen in step 3. Donors are taken in ascending fixture-id order.
    3. Screen scorerlens-r4-plus-containment-v1 — literally leading's equivalent_claim: rung-4 equivalence under scorerlens.rung_match, or folded containment either way (NFC + casefold + strip). What the screen does not prove: paraphrases outside the ladder's grammar still pass — "1/2" against "0.5" is the named example (the frozen numeric grammar is ASCII-decimal; fraction notation is outside it). The screen also has a stated cost: containment removes the best legitimate distractors (donor "Paris, Texas" against true "Paris" is a genuinely different, maximally plausible choice). The screen is adopted anyway — ambiguity in the correctness definition is worse than an easier set, and the resulting upward bias is disclosed by the strata in §7.
    4. Dedup. Donors are deduplicated by byte identity, then by rung-4 equivalence class, in pool order: a donor whose class is already represented in the set is skipped. The realised set is the true choice plus the first three distinct-class eligible donors.
    5. Shortfall. If fewer than three distinct-class eligible donors exist, the fixture is not constructible: it enters no choice population for any candidate, and the shortfall is counted in the per-fixture eligibility record. There is no numeric-perturbation fallback (leading's digit surgery is rejected on the record: a fabricated string is not a plausible choice the way it is a plausible claim, and the fallback would relieve shortfall selectively for numeric fixtures — a difficulty-correlated denominator distortion).

    The construction is recorded per fixture in the payload (fixtures block: target id and group, exclusion reason if any, the chosen donors' fixture ids and groups, a SHA-256 digest of every choice text, the realised distinct-choice count, constructible or not) and bound by donor_map_sha256 over the whole block (leading's precedent: editing fixture B's expected changes fixture A's choice set, so the map is part of the treatment and is digested as such). Choice order is frozen — true choice first, then donors in ascending fixture-id order — and is presentational only, since each choice is scored in its own forward pass.

    4. Rankings (three, one headline)¶

    Per fixture and candidate, each choice carries the engine's reported values: float32-rounded total log-likelihood, float32-rounded mean per scored token, scored token count, and target byte length (TargetScore already reports all four).

    • Total (headline): argmax over reported totals. This is lm-eval's acc semantics exactly (EleutherAI, https://blog.eleuther.ai/multiple-choice-normalization/).
    • Per-byte: score defined as reported total ÷ target byte length, computed from the reported values so producer and validator agree bit-for-bit. This is lm-eval's acc_norm semantics (byte-length-normalised).
    • Per-token mean: argmax over reported means. This is the token-length-normalised quantity the harness deliberately does not report (it is not tokenization-agnostic). It is reported because the engine already produces it and the comparison here is intra-checkpoint (one tokenizer), but it must never be labelled acc_norm in any surface — payload, report, docs, or CLI help.

    The reported mean is taken from the engine's reported means directly. It is not derivable from the reported total (the engine rounds the mean from the unrounded total), and no code — producer, validator, or test oracle — may exact-check mean == total / count or total == sum(per_token); either check manufactures phantom drift on honest payloads. Per-token values are not stored at all.

    The audit measures total-vs-normalised disagreement; it applies no PMI or calibration correction (Holtzman et al., arXiv: 2104.08315, surface-form competition, is the cited reason such corrections exist). A discordant cell is evidence about the scoring rule, not a repaired score.

    5. Cells, evaluability, ties¶

    • Cell. One (candidate, fixture, choice) teacher-forced score. The prompt span is alignment-checked once per (candidate, fixture) (check_alignment over the prompt with an empty target): a prompt-side failure (decode_failed, encode_failed, prompt_roundtrip_mismatch from that pre-check) voids all four cells with that reason, is counted once per fixture in n_prompt_failures, and reserves the per-cell reasons for target-side causes (target roundtrip, boundary merge, provider and context codes — including context_limit_exceeded, a first-class counted reason).
    • Non-finite demotion. A cell whose reported total or mean is non-finite (reachable with status ok: float32 saturation of an extreme finite logit sum — the cycle-76 pin) is stored as unavailable with the fixed reason non_finite_score, in producer and validator vocabularies alike. A verbatim -inf would crash the allow_nan=False report write; the shipped logprob diagnostic's per-fixture path carries this latent exposure today and the audit does not inherit it.
    • Evaluability. A fixture has a choice verdict under a candidate iff it is constructible and every constructed cell has status exactly ok with finite reported values. (ok_empty is a distinct engine status, not ok: strip-empty choices never reach scoring because construction excludes them, so an ok_empty cell appearing anyway is a protocol violation, counted under its own reason, never folded into alignment unavailability.) Anything else is choice_unavailable for that candidate, with per-reason cell counts beside it. Partial choice sets are never argmaxed: unavailability correlates with the choice's surface form and with the candidate's rendering, so a surviving-subset argmax is an easier, different task per fixture.
    • Ties. Per fixture and ranking, the verdict is correct (true choice uniquely at the reported maximum), tied (true choice at the maximum with at least one other choice), or incorrect (otherwise). Ties are counted, never broken, and never counted as correct. All argmax comparisons — producer and validator — operate on the reported (float32-rounded) values, so a tie is a property of the published numbers, not of hidden precision.

    6. Populations, denominators, the method-level headline¶

    • Per-candidate accuracies are reported beside their own denominators (evaluable fixtures for that candidate). A candidate with zero evaluable fixtures reports accuracy None with a fixed detail — never 0.0, never a crash (scorer-lens precedent).
    • The method-level comparison is computed only over the common evaluable population: fixtures with a choice verdict under every candidate. n_common is an always-reported readout, next to the floor outcome — never a silent absence.
    • The floor is the probe-local constant MIN_COMMON_EVALUABLE = 6. Below it, the agreement claim is withheld with the fixed detail choiceaudit_common_population_below_floor; the counts still print. The floor gates the claim, never the reporting. Note the compounded arithmetic (B-NEW-3): all-cells evaluability intersected across up to 24 candidates makes a withheld headline a routine, expected outcome on boundary-hostile tokenizers — a known property of the instrument, stated here so it is never mistaken for a defect.
    • Native comparator. The run's primary outcome is mapped exactly as leading maps it: compatible_on_fixture_set and held_out_failed map to (selected, selected_candidate); any other decision passes through with its winner (usually None). The audit is gated on a complete search before any scoring: decision in {failed, cancelled}, a missing search list, or a search list shorter than the candidate list yields the whole payload {"available": False, "detail": "native_search_incomplete"} (leading's wrapper gate, same detail string).
    • Agreement is multi-state, never a bare boolean, with frozen priority: withheld (below floor) → native_abstain (mapped primary decision is not a selection) → choice_tied (choice accuracies tie at the top over the common population) → agree / disagree.

    7. Discordance and strata¶

    • Generation verdict per (candidate, fixture), from the recorded observation's stored facts: pass iff the observation is present with status completed and exact_match and boundary_found; fail iff completed and not both; missing otherwise (no observation, or not completed), counted as n_generation_missing. (In genuine runs exact_match is construction-gated on completion at core.py's single observation site; the status check is kept anyway so a foreign payload cannot convert a missing measurement into a generation fail.) Records store the facts (status, exact_match, boundary_found); the verdict is derived identically by producer and validator.
    • The 2×2 per candidate, headline (total) ranking, over fixtures with a generation verdict and a choice verdict of correct or incorrect: all four cells printed (gen-pass/choice-correct, gen-pass/choice-incorrect, gen-fail/choice-correct, gen-fail/choice-incorrect). Tied choice verdicts are counted beside the table, never forced into it. The generation-fail row is split by boundary_found into labelled sub-counts. The word "boundary" names two different facts in this payload — generation boundary_found (the response terminator was found) and the scoring reason boundary_merge_mismatch (a token straddles the prompt/answer seam) — and the report glosses the seam reason once as tokenisation alignment so the two are never read as one. Boundary-caused discordance is a named component of discordance, reported, not discounted: termination behaviour is part of what free generation does and teacher forcing does not.
    • Strata per candidate (total ranking, zero extra forwards): accuracy split by numeric vs non-numeric true answer (the ladder's numeric grammar decides), accuracy on the token-length-matched subset (every donor within ±1 scored tokens of the true answer's scored token count), and the per-ranking verdict-agreement counts (total vs per-byte, total vs per-token mean). Donors are unmatched — answers to unrelated fixtures, screened for equivalence only, not for plausibility, type, or length — so choice accuracy is an upper bound on item-matched choice accuracy, and the design says so wherever the number is introduced.

    8. Ground records and the diff validator¶

    The payload is self-contained (the default report view strips observations, so nothing may be joined back at diff time): protocol object (version, donor rule, screen name, K, ranking names, donor_map_sha256, backend fingerprint), the fixtures construction block (§3), and per-candidate entries carrying status/detail, the aggregates of §6–§7, and one record per search fixture: fixture id, the generation facts, the prompt reason if any, and the four cells (status, reason, reported total, reported mean, scored token count, target byte length — no per-token values).

    The diff validator (diff_choiceaudit) recomputes every derived value from these ground records with its own mirrored implementation (diffreports loads standalone; it imports nothing from the producer): the donor-map digest over the stored block, donor distinctness and cross-group membership from the stored ids/groups, per-ranking verdicts and tie classification from the reported values, accuracies, the 2×2 and its boundary split, the strata, the common population, and the method-level agreement including its withholding. It asserts every stored float is float32-representable (mirrored struct check) — recompute from reported values is only meaningful if the stored values are the reported values. It never derives a mean from a total.

    What the validator does not verify, stated plainly: the equivalence screen and donor provenance against the fixture texts (records carry digests, not texts — the same binding standard as the shipped scorer-lens and leading validators, which verify binding and arithmetic over producer-asserted ground records). Protocol, donor-map, or backend drift between two payloads marks the comparison not comparable, with the drift still listed.

    9. Cost, caps, cancellation¶

    • Cost: 4 forward passes per candidate per fixture (9,600 at the legal maximum of 24 candidates × 100 search fixtures), stated in the CLI help in the tree's cost-sentence form. A scoring forward is one pass; this is one to two orders of magnitude cheaper than a generation probe's budget at the same caps, and cheaper than the native search the run already paid for.
    • Caps, checked before any scoring (the house pattern): records = candidates × search fixtures ≤ MAX_RECORDS = 2400 (fits the legal maximum exactly — no legal scale is made unavailable) and a 12 MiB records byte cap; breach fails closed with choiceaudit_cell_budget_exceeded.
    • Cancellation is checked at each fixture boundary (per-cell checks are permitted, not required); a cancelled audit is {"available": False, "detail": "cancelled"} with no partial aggregates.
    • Backends without forward_logits (everything except LocalHFBackend today) yield the whole audit unavailable with the engine's logits_not_supported detail — honest degradation, and the dispatch tests are built for it.

    10. Vocabulary and boundaries¶

    Second-opinion vocabulary, frozen: ranking keys live under the choice_audit payload; the native comparator is always named primary_*; the HTML section is titled "Choice-scoring audit (second opinion)". The tree's discipline is namespacing, not word bans (scorer-lens emits winner_by_rung inside its own payload; leading emits per-condition decision/winner beside primary_*) — the audit follows it. Two boundary sentences, identical in this doc, USAGE, and CLI help:

    • Against --logprob-diagnostic: the diagnostic teacher-forces only each fixture's expected answer and bands its mean logprob beside the native verdicts; it constructs no choice set, ranks nothing, and cannot express that another scoring method would have picked a different answer or template.
    • Against --scorer-lens: the lens re-grades the recorded generations under normalisation rungs and re-derives a winner from the same answers; the audit scores answers the model never generated and compares the two methods' winners.

    11. Uniqueness (research pass, cycle 195)¶

    Log-likelihood choice scoring is established prior art — it is lm-eval's native multiple-choice mode (acc over summed log-probability; acc_norm over byte-length-normalised log-probability; EleutherAI, https://blog.eleuther.ai/multiple-choice-normalization/), and HELM showed that the choice between likelihood scoring (separate) and generating the answer (joint) can swing accuracy by tens of points across models under a standardised prompt (Liang et al., arXiv:2211.09110). What no surveyed tool does is run both scoring ontologies over the same hand-rendered chat-template candidates on a single local checkpoint — greedy generation judged by exact match plus response boundary under the lab's conservative unique-winner/abstention rule on one side, and teacher-forced choice scoring over donor-screened choice sets built from the run's own fixtures on the other — and audit their disagreement per fixture (the full generation × choice 2×2) and at the method level (whether the two methods' top candidates agree, ties reported as ties). The combination — donor-screened choice sets × template family × generation-winner discordance audit — is the claim. Nearest neighbours, each missing at least two limbs: HELM's adaptation comparison (across models, dataset- native choice sets, no abstention winner), Molfese et al. (arXiv:2503.14996 — its "Logprobs" is the answer letter token's probability, not full-string scoring), and the MCQ vs free-generation comparison of arXiv:2403.17752 (a study, not a tool; no template candidates, no winner audited).

    12. Falsification¶

    Zero discordance on completed, fully-evaluable cells under the total-loglikelihood ranking — over the reported completed-cell denominator, for greedy generation, this checkpoint, and this fixture set — falsifies "the scoring method matters for this checkpoint/fixture set": a publishable null that cross-validates the native winner. An audit whose 2×2 population is empty claims no falsification; it reports its denominators.
